## Rationale

Executor scored 40.8% on a discovery / identify-all fraud-detection task (8 of 18 fraud payments recovered, 0 false positives). The Executor correctly enumerated multiple signal families per rule 2 and found two non-empty signals: (a) impossible-travel pairs producing 8 records (same payment_method_fingerprint reused at two physically distant stores within minutes — shape-1 shared value AND shape-2 per-record location-vs-store violation); (b) geographic implausibility producing 16 different records (observation hundreds of km from the store the payment claims, with a sharp natural break in the bucketed distance distribution: 815/38/149/16). Members of (b) violate the same per-record cross-field invariant (observed location inconsistent with own store_id), but they share no cross-record value (different customers, months, stores). The Executor read v0007's rule 4 ('Discrete identifier > fuzzy threshold ... do not submit a threshold-only candidate set whose members share no exact identifier ... treat the threshold matches as supporting evidence around a discrete-signal core rather than as the answer') as licence to drop (b) entirely and submit only (a), explicitly logging the 16 records as 'noise (GPS drift, online purchases attributed to physical store, customers who live far from the store they visit).' This is the exact under-report failure that v0007's own rollback note predicted: 'the grader flags an answer that dropped clear geographic outliers because no discrete identifier could be confirmed.' Root cause: v0007's rule 4 conflates two senses of 'discrete identifier' — a cross-record shared value AND a per-record invariant — and only the first sense is illustrated in the rule text, so the Executor treated 'no shared value across members' as 'this signal is noise.' Fix: rewrite rule 4 to make the three discrete-invariant shapes explicit and equally trustworthy — (1) cross-record shared value, (2) per-record cross-field invariant violation (the cohort shares the violated *rule*, not a value), (3) sharp natural break in a bucketed distribution; add a worked example using the trial's exact distance-bucket numbers to anchor shape (3); extend rule 2 with one sentence stating that when two signals both pass the rule-4 trust check and flag different records, the answer is the union of both; extend the low-recall failure-mode definition at the section opening to name this exact path ('discarding one signal family's matches because another family produced a tighter-correlated set'); extend the coverage check (rule 7) to require recording which of the three shapes each firing signal satisfies and to require including the matches of every discrete signal that fired, not only the tightest one; update rule 6 and the 'threshold-only candidate set' anti-pattern to reference the three-shape framing; add a new anti-pattern targeting the exact failure ('Treating no shared cross-record value as proof a signal is noise, and discarding the matches of one signal family because another signal had a tighter cross-record correlate'). Generalises to any per-record cross-field invariant (amount vs line sum, observation vs claimed location, status vs workflow-state column) regardless of trial topic.
